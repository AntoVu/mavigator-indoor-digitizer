Indoor Digitizer

Pin UTA's evacuation diagrams onto a building's outline, one floor at a time, and trace the hallways, rooms, stairs, entrances and useful spots over them. Any building can get entrances, so routes end at a real door. It saves indoor-edits.json, which npm run import:osm turns into the routing graph.

Draft kept in this browser
How this works
  1. Run npm run fetch:evac. It downloads the diagrams into tools/indoor/cache/evac/. They are copyrighted: they stay on your computer and in this browser, and are never saved to the draft or the repo.
  2. Add folder and pick tools/indoor/cache/evac/ once. Every diagram is filed under its building and floor and kept in this browser, so you don't add them again next time.
  3. Click a building on the campus map. Nedderman, ERB and Woolf are labeled, but any building opens, even just to mark its entrances.
  4. Each floor's diagrams are grouped into views: diagrams that differ only in the red "you are here" dot are one view. Press Use as layout on each view and Align it: click a corner on the diagram, then the same corner of the outline. Three pairs place it; a fourth shows the fit in meters. Or press Place by hand and drag the diagram itself over the building: inside it to move it, a corner to resize it, an edge’s middle dot to stretch it, the round knob to rotate it. Rotate the diagram under it if it is drawn turned. For a point nothing marks, draw rulers (R) on the diagram or the map: guide lines that hold Shift for 45° steps, and clicks snap where they cross. If a grouping is wrong, move a diagram to another view.
  5. Upper floor smaller than the building, or OSM's shape off? Floor outline: drag corners, click an edge to add one, right-click a corner to delete it. Wings that don't connect on a floor? Cut out a gap: box the space between them, across the outer wall, and the outline splits into pieces (Add a piece and Delete this piece work on pieces directly). The Building outline button beside the floors edits the building's own shape, which is not a floor: it replaces OSM's here at once and on the campus map after npm run import:osm, and is the walls of any floor without its own outline. Combine all floors builds it from every floor's outline. Snap 45° keeps corners, hallways, room walls, areas, door lines, gate bars and Move drags square with the building; hold Shift to flip it. Snap to features makes clicks and drags land on nearby hallways, room corners, walls and edges (a red ring shows where); uncheck it to place freely. Building drawn a few degrees off north? Level to a wall, then click a wall of the outline: the view turns so it is level, and room boxes, snaps and guides square to it. It only changes the view (North up undoes it); nothing saved moves.
  6. Trace: hallway centerlines (they join where they meet), a door for each room with its number (a door between two rooms, like an office inside another room, is a line drawn across the doorway, labeled on each side), objects (vending machines, seating...) and entrances, which snap onto the wall. Each entrance says who may go in (anyone, staff with a keycard, or no one) and out; tick Emergency exit for alarmed doors, which routes never use. A gate (the Gate button) is a door across a hallway with a rule each way: click the hallway on one side of it, then the other, and pick who may pass each way (a keycard door, a one-way exit, an emergency door). Hallways only reachable through it are tinted its color; drag its bar's ends to the walls. Room, restroom, stair and elevator doors take the same rules: who may go in, who may come out, and Emergency. An ordinary door anyone opens needs nothing: just trace the hallway through it. Areas are commons, lobbies and atriums: everything on an area's edge joins in straight lines, so there is no need for rows of parallel hallways.
  7. Room outlines are what the app draws, so it shows a floor plan instead of hallway lines. Click one corner of a room, then the opposite corner: the box lines up with the building, and its corners snap to other rooms and the floor outline. If the box touches one room’s door, it takes that room’s number. Check People may walk through on a lecture hall or library to let routes cross it. The Type picker also draws a restroom, stairwell or elevator (box its walls, then click its entrance; each has its own color) and solid blocks for pillars and filled-in walls. A stairwell or elevator lists the floors it goes to in its panel: checking one copies its walls and entrance there, to drag into place. Then click the room to shape it: drag corners, click an edge to add a corner, drag an edge’s middle dot to curve it, right-click a corner to delete it.
  8. Move: drag a door, entrance, object, stairwell, hallway or room to move it. Drag on an empty spot to box an area, then drag the selection to move everything inside it together, or drag a corner square of its dashed box to resize it all at once (hold Shift to keep its proportions). Space+drag pans. Each diagram has its own opacity slider in its row under Diagrams.
  9. Undo with Ctrl+Z and redo with Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z), or the buttons by Fit: any change, from a dragged corner to an opened file. While drawing (a hallway, an area, a room box), Ctrl+Z takes back the last point first.
  10. Save indoor-edits.json into src/data/, run npm run import:osm, then npm test. The import report's INDOOR section lists anything it could not place.
